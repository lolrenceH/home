abstract: Microglia, the brain's resident immune cells, undergo significant changes during neurodegeneration, adopting disease-associated phenotypes. This study examines the enrichment of neurodegenerative microglia signatures in brain-derived extracellular vesicles isolated from Alzheimer's disease mouse models. Using quantitative proteomics, we identified specific protein signatures associated with activated microglia and demonstrated how these signatures are reflected in extracellular vesicles. These findings provide insights into microglial activation in Alzheimer's disease and suggest that extracellular vesicles may serve as biomarkers for neuroinflammation and disease progression.
