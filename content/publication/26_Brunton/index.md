abstract: Animal intelligence is not purely a product of abstract computation in the brain; it emerges from dynamic interactions between the nervous system and the body. New connectome datasets and musculoskeletal body models make it possible to build integrated, closed-loop simulations of embodied behavior, but how much biological meaning such simulations carry is unclear. Here we build a deliberately mismatched virtual chimera, the digital sphinx, by coupling the connectome of the nematode C. elegans to a biomechanical model of the fruit fly Drosophila. The worm connectome receives sensory signals from the fly body, and an artificial neural network trained with deep reinforcement learning maps worm motor neuron activity onto the fly's leg actuators. The resulting agent walks realistically even though the identities of its neurons have no biological meaning for the fly body. Behavioral fidelity can therefore be achieved without biological fidelity, which makes connectome-body simulations easy to overinterpret and motivates stronger tests of biological validity.
